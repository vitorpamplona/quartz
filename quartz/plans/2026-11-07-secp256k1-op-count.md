# secp256k1: operation counts per call, and where the work goes

Status: measured, no changes yet. Counted with temporary counters on every `FieldP` mul/sqr/inv/sqrt
and add/sub/neg/half, the three point operations and `ScalarN` mul/inv (branch
`claude/opcount-instrumentation`, test `OpCountReport`), averaged over 40 random keys after warm-up.

## Measured

Field multiplies + squares per call (the cost driver; native field multiply runs at C speed):

| Call | mul | sqr | total | of which inversions / sqrt | point ops |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pubkeyCreate` | 365 | 395 | 760 | 1 inv (270, 36%) | 43 addMixed, 3 dbl |
| `signSchnorrWithXOnlyPubKey` | 364 | 394 | 758 | 1 inv (36%) | 43 addMixed, 3 dbl |
| `signSchnorr` (derives the pubkey again) | 731 | 790 | 1,521 | 2 inv | 2x the above |
| `verifySchnorr`, pubkey seen before | 897 | 949 | 1,846 | 1 inv (15%) | 127 dbl, 64 addMixed |
| `verifySchnorr`, new pubkey | 1,079 | 1,507 | 2,586 | 2 inv + 1 sqrt (31%) | + 7 full adds (table) |
| `ecdhXOnly` | 912 | 1,441 | 2,353 | 2 inv + 1 sqrt (34%) | 127 dbl, 44 addMixed, 7 adds |
| `pubKeyTweakMul` | 1,265 | 1,584 | 2,849 | 3 inv | includes a `pubkeyCreate` in the test |
| `verifySchnorrBatch`, per event, n=200 | 27 | 261 | 288 | 1 sqrt (92%) | 1.3 addMixed |

One inversion (addition chain, 255 sqr + 15 mul) is 270 field operations; one square root is 266.
Verify also does about as many cheap operations as multiplies: 1,006 add, 439 sub, 283 neg, 126 half.

## Compared with libsecp256k1's algorithms

From libsecp256k1's source (master, 2026-11): doubling 3M+4S, mixed add 8M+3S, `ECMULT_WINDOW_SIZE`
15 for G, `WINDOW_A` 5 with GLV for P, comb 11 blocks x 6 teeth for `ecmult_gen` (the same comb
Quartz uses).

- **The curve arithmetic matches.** 127 doublings and ~64 mixed additions per verify is what
  libsecp256k1's Strauss/wNAF does (it saves ~4 additions with its larger G table). `mulG` is the
  same comb. No algorithmic headroom there.
- **Inversions are the difference.** libsecp256k1 inverts with safegcd (Bernstein-Yang, `modinv64`),
  variable-time where inputs are public, and builds the P table with a shared Z ("global z") so it
  needs no inversion there at all. Quartz uses the 270-operation exponentiation chain everywhere:
  - verify: 1 inversion (the y-parity check needs affine y); new pubkey: +1 for the affine P table
  - pubkey / sign: 1 inversion (36% of the work)
  - ECDH: 2 inversions + 1 sqrt (the sqrt is needed: x-only key)
- **Batch verify is all square roots.** 92% of its per-event work is lifting R from x (a sqrt per
  event), which no algorithm avoids.

## Correction to earlier notes

The native "sign 24.9 us vs C 16.3 us" comparison was `signSchnorr` (two `mulG`: it re-derives the
pubkey) against libsecp256k1 signing with a cached keypair (one `ecmult_gen`). With the pubkey passed
in, Quartz does half the work; on the JVM `signSchnorr (XPubKey)` already beats ACINQ (40-46k vs
30-34k ops/s). `Nip01Crypto.sign` without a pubkey takes the slow path.

## Bug: batch verify accepts invalid signatures

`verifySchnorrBatch` checks `(sum s_i)G - (sum e_i)P = sum R_i` with no random weights, so errors
cancel: from two valid signatures by the same key, `(R1, s1 + d)` and `(R2, s2 - d)` are both
invalid and the batch accepts them. `OpCountReport.batchAcceptsCancellingInvalidSignatures` shows
it (each fails `verifySchnorr`; the batch returns true). Anyone holding two of a user's events can
do it, e.g. a relay. It cannot forge new content (that still needs the discrete log of P), but the
doc comment's "errors cannot cancel" is wrong. BIP-340's batch verification multiplies each equation
by a random 128-bit coefficient `a_i` (a_1 = 1): `(sum a_i s_i)G - (sum a_i e_i)P - sum a_i R_i = O`.
That adds a multi-scalar multiplication over the R_i with 128-bit scalars, roughly 100-200 field
operations per event with Pippenger at n = 200 (estimate), on top of today's 288.

## Candidates, by expected gain

1. Fix batch verify (random weights). Correctness first; keeps most of the batch advantage.
2. Variable-time safegcd inversion for field and scalar. Removes most of 270 operations per
   inversion: pubkey/sign ~-25-30%, ECDH and new-pubkey verify ~-20%, cached verify ~-10% (to be
   measured: safegcd also needs 128-bit products).
3. Global-z P tables in verify (new pubkey) and ECDH: one inversion fewer each (~-10%).
4. Time the cheap operations on the JVM (as many as the multiplies; 4x64 add/sub carry and reduce
   every time, where libsecp256k1's 5x52 adds lazily).
5. G window 12 -> 15: ~4 additions per verify, <3%. Not worth the 8x table.
